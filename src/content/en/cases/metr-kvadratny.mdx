## An end-to-end user journey across products

Using a successful scenario for two products out of five as an example

<Flow>

I started by collecting data about the products: how they worked and what made each of them different. We paid special attention to the points where the products touched. This stage required close communication with stakeholders and the team. Several times a week we held product sessions where we discussed how the products worked and gathered information about what development could and couldn’t do.

We found that mobile users spend 5 to 25 minutes per session with the product on average, mostly in the morning and evening. More than 42% of them come back to search at least three times before contacting the owner. Funnel analysis showed that, without a single user journey, moving between the key stages — from finding a property to the mortgage and the deal — came with a conversion drop of up to 35%.

</Flow>

<UserPath />

<Flow>

Search looks simple at first glance, but huge result lists slow down the choice, and overloaded filters get in the way. We rebuilt the filter, keeping only parameters that cut more than 60% of irrelevant listings — more of an axe than a scalpel.

Already in the MVP we shipped Saved search and Quick match, which capture the user’s intent and let them come back to it without setting everything up again. All of this sped up repeat sessions and made it easier to find the right property.

</Flow>

## Then we moved on to the Mortgage service

P.S. It was the main service, since we worked under a bank

<Flow>

Then negotiations began with the mortgage provider. Our goal was obvious: simplify the application form as much as possible to raise conversion. According to UX research, every extra field in a form lowers conversion by 5–10%, and long forms on mobile devices are a key reason people abandon them.

Resistance from the provider was expected: compliance requirements and internal scoring models left almost no room to manoeuvre. A battle for every field began. We argued for a shorter form using competitors’ experience, the impact of form length on the funnel and user scenarios. We found a compromise by splitting the data: the critical fields on the first step, the rest after pre-approval.

</Flow>

## We delivered on our goals

But that was only the beginning: we were then trusted to keep growing the product

<Flow>

In 4 months we launched a mobile product that brought five disparate business units together into a single real-estate ecosystem. We focused on metrics rather than the amount of functionality:

- Ran 20+ product sessions with stakeholders and the development team
- Cut the MVP scope by 35%, speeding up time-to-market
- Shortened the mortgage form by 40%, which raised the completion rate by 18%
- Optimised search — the filter began cutting out more than 60% of irrelevant listings, reducing time-to-action by 22%
- Introduced Saved search, increasing retention by 27%
- Produced a full set of design artefacts: user flows, a UI kit, interactive prototypes
- Reduced uncertainty during development and sped up delivery thanks to thoroughly designed scenarios
- Held several design reviews, keeping design and development in sync
- Successfully published the app on the App Store and Google Play

It wasn’t just an app launch. It was a shift from a set of services to an ecosystem model able to grow user LTV through cross-unit scenarios: from search to mortgage, from the deal to renovation and moving.
