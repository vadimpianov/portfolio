I joined the project in the middle of a large-scale transformation: the business expected systemic change, not cosmetic tweaks. At the same time the company had no design culture — a designer was seen as just a pair of hands. There was no shared visual language either: every platform — mobile products, web and CRM — had its own UI and its own logic.

Drawing on my experience in betting, I focused on reworking the key revenue scenarios:

- **Registration** — the first point of churn and the start of LTV
- **Deposit** — conversion into a paying user
- **Bet slip** — the decision point and growth of the average bet

## Registration: before and after

### Before

<Columns>
<div>

The “old” registration was a long quest: several screens of mandatory fields — citizenship, date of birth, promo code, phone, email, password and SMS confirmation. Many of these fields weren't required by the system and could be moved inside the app: the promo code into the account area, the date of birth into identity verification, and citizenship could go entirely, leaving only residency.

</div>
<div>

Registration took more than two minutes, and every extra step cut conversion by up to 7% while pushing the drop-off risk up to 40%. It's important that 70% of new users were already verified with TsUPIS (the Russian betting regulator's registry) and could go straight to betting. The other 30% — new, unique users — were not: they were complete newcomers to betting. Keeping this cohort was a special challenge for us.

</div>
</Columns>

### After

<Columns>
<div>

After studying competitors and adjacent industries, I introduced two new ways to sign up — via bank ID. This type of sign-in/sign-up cuts registration and identity verification from 5 minutes down to 1–2 and can lift conversion by 15–25%. The user confirms their details in the bank's app and returns to the product already verified — no manual form filling.

</div>
<div>

Instead of a login and password, we built sign-in by phone number — the familiar flow from most popular apps. The user enters their number, gets an SMS code and is in within 10–20 seconds. Research shows that up to 15% of support requests are about password recovery; OTP sign-in reduces that flow and speeds up access.

</div>
</Columns>

It sounds simple, but there was a lot of work behind it: we had to gather information, explore the APIs, account for the regulator's (TsUPIS) constraints and untangle the old registration code.

The process, built on design thinking principles, involved the whole product team — designers, analysts and developers. Together we identified risks, tested hypotheses and negotiated trade-offs between UX, technical constraints and regulatory requirements. Before touching the mockups, we held more than 30 meetings with the business analyst, bank teams and TsUPIS.

We went through dozens of pages of technical documentation to ensure correct user identification and full regulatory compliance.

</Flow>

## More about the process

<Columns>
<div>

To get there, we analysed competitors, studied backend and regulatory constraints, layered behavioural analytics onto the mind map and tested hypotheses against historical data. Then we aligned the final vision with the product team and stakeholders.

When developing new features, we run regular product meetings with all key participants — product, analytics, engineering, marketing and business — to keep metrics, risks and priorities in sync.

Every feature goes through aligning hypotheses and success metrics, identifying risks and technical constraints, defining the MVP and priorities, and recording artefacts and areas of responsibility.

This format reduces rework, speeds up decisions, surfaces bottlenecks early and keeps the whole team engaged and focused on the outcome rather than the process.

As a result, we don't just “hand design over to development” — we work as one product team with shared responsibility for metrics and results.

</div>

## We delivered on our goals

Conversion to registration and deposit went up

<Columns>
<div>

In 3 months we rebuilt registration: studied TsUPIS requirements and constraints, united the team — product managers, analysts, engineering and business — around one goal, higher conversion, and shipped the app to the App Store and Google Play.

We focused on simplification and growth:

- Cut the number of mandatory steps and moved secondary data into the signed-in area
- Removed everything that restricted access to betting
- Built one-click registration for 70% of new app users and simplified identity verification for the 30% who were new to the industry
- Reduced registration time from over two minutes to under one

</div>
<div>

- Reduced drop-off by 26%
- Increased conversion to completed registration and first deposit by 26% and 10% respectively
- Increased conversion to deposit by 8%
- Reduced the load on support thanks to a clearer, simpler flow

This wasn't just a registration redesign. It was a shift from a formal compliance process to a product model, where registration stops being a barrier and becomes an entry point.

As always, we produced a full set of design artefacts — CJM, user flows, prototypes, error scenarios and edge cases — which removed uncertainty in development and sped up delivery.

</div>
