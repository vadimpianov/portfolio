I joined the project in the middle of a large-scale transformation: the business expected systemic change, not cosmetic tweaks. At the same time the company had no design culture — a designer was seen as just a pair of hands. There was no shared visual language either: every platform — mobile products, web and CRM — had its own UI and its own logic.

Drawing on my experience in betting, I focused on reworking the key revenue scenarios:

- **Registration** — the first point of churn and the start of LTV
- **Deposit** — conversion into a paying user
- **Bet slip** — the decision point and growth of the average bet

## Registration: before and after

### Before

<Flow>

The “old” registration was a long quest: several screens of mandatory fields — citizenship, date of birth, promo code, phone, email, password and SMS confirmation. Many of them weren't needed at sign-up: the promo code could be entered in the account area, the date of birth during identity verification, and citizenship replaced with residency.

Registration took more than two minutes, and up to five together with identity verification. According to industry data, every extra step cuts conversion by 7%, and the drop-off rate can reach 40%. Meanwhile, 70% of new users were already verified with TsUPIS (the Russian betting regulator's registry) — all they needed was a fast sign-up. The other 30% were new to betting and had to go through identity verification from scratch — for them, the key was to make that path clear.

</Flow>

### After

<Flow>

After studying competitors and adjacent industries, I initiated the development of sign-up via bank ID. The user confirms their details in the bank's app and returns to the product already verified — no manual form filling. On the prototype, registration and identity verification time dropped from 5 minutes to 1–2, and according to the providers, this kind of sign-in lifts conversion by 15–25%.

Instead of a login and password, we built sign-in by phone number — the familiar flow from most popular apps. The user enters their number, gets an SMS code and is in within 10–20 seconds. Research shows that up to 15% of support requests are about password recovery; OTP sign-in reduces that flow and speeds up access.

</Flow>

## More about the process

<Columns>
<div>

It sounds simple, but there was a lot of work behind it. We held more than 30 meetings with the business analyst, bank teams and TsUPIS. We went through dozens of pages of technical documentation, the APIs and the backend and regulatory constraints, and together with the developers figured out how the old registration worked. I then cross-checked the mind map against behavioural analytics and tested hypotheses on historical data, and we agreed on the final vision with the product team and stakeholders.

I built a process grounded in design thinking principles and brought the whole team into it — product managers, analytics, development, marketing and business. For every feature we aligned hypotheses and success metrics, identified risks and technical constraints, defined the MVP and priorities, and recorded artefacts and ownership. That means less rework and faster decisions: design isn't just “handed off to development” — the team shares responsibility for the metrics and the result.

## I delivered on the goals

Conversion to registration and deposit went up

<Flow>

In 3 months we rebuilt registration: studied TsUPIS requirements and constraints. United the team around one goal — higher conversion, and shipped the app to the stores.

I focused on simplification and growth:

- Cut the number of mandatory steps and moved secondary data into the signed-in area
- Removed everything that restricted access to betting
- Built one-click registration for 70% of new app users and simplified identity verification for the 30% who were new to the industry
- Reduced registration time from over two minutes to under one

{/* */}

- Reduced drop-off by 26%
- Increased conversion to completed registration and first deposit by 26% and 10% respectively
- Reduced the load on support thanks to a clearer, simpler flow

This wasn't just a registration redesign. It was a shift from a formal compliance process to a product model, where registration stops being a barrier and becomes the entry point to the product.

As always, I produced a full set of design artefacts — CJM, user flows, prototypes, error scenarios and edge cases — which removed uncertainty in development and sped up delivery.

</Flow>
